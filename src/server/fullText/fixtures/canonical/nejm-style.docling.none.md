ABSTRACT BACKGROUND The effect of restricting intravenous fluid in adults with septic shock is uncertain. METHODS We randomly assigned 1554 adults with septic shock to restrictive or liberal fluid therapy. The primary outcome was death at 90 days. RESULTS Death at 90 days occurred in 42.3% of the patients in the restrictive group and in 42.1% in the liberal group. CONCLUSIONS Restrictive fluid therapy did not reduce mortality at 90 days. (Funded by the Nordic Research Council; FLUID-S number, NCT09999999.)

Septic shock affects more than 1.5 million patients each year, and intravenous fluid remains the first treatment given.

Liberal fluid strategies were standard at Karolinska University Hospital and most other centers until 2020, when two pilot trials suggested harm from fluid accumulation.

We conducted a multicenter trial to compare restrictive and liberal fluid therapy in adults with septic shock.

## Methods

The trial was conducted at 31 intensive care units in 8 countries.
